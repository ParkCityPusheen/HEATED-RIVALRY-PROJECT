The days snail by so peacefully after Dan's discharge that he gets whiplash.

His first day away, he attempts once to return the money Mr. Joo has deposited into his bank account. Firstly, Dan calculates the decades it would take to return the money needed to cover his debt, that Mr. Joo mentioned handling previously as part of their agreement and he realizes now he should've argued against it. Naturally it's unsuccessful and Dan ends up having to speak with the bank due to the rubber-banding of such a large number of funds. Dan concedes, as usual, and continues on with his day.

Waking up isn't a task to check off now, there's no list of notable goals needing adhering to now, so Dan comes to slow and sluggishly. He makes himself coffee, drinks it first while staring out of the kitchen window, then brushes his teeth and has a lukewarm shower. He unpacks, and while surfing the internet on his laptop (purchased by Mr. Joo Jaekyung) falls right back to sleep in the intoxicating muteness of his newly attained life.

When he comes to, Dan ventures out to the open market on the boardwalk, eyes closed and listening to the waves crashing along the shore once arriving. He's not greedy, but he allows himself basic human decencies; he purchases fresh mackerel and tinned fish, smoked and packed in a spicy curry locally. Dan eats quickly, cleans just as so, takes a long and hot shower and falls asleep as soon as he lies his head on his pillow counting the phantom pains he feels from his wound.

He dreams of broad shoulders and an even wider back and tells himself he doesn't know who it is when he finally wakes up.

The second day he realizes his payment for the services he provided Mr. Joo are *triple* what they should be so he sucks in a sharp breath, ready to play transfer-funds-tag with the professional athlete once more. This time, however, Dan finds out that Mr. Joo has deactivated his account with said bank so it's impossible to even attempt a return.

The third day it pours, all day, and while watching his grandmother sleep he remembers he should be paying for her stay at the hospice facility and shuffles towards the nurses' desk to do so. Only, one Joo Jaekyung has already assumed his grandmother's payments:

"I wish I could change the primary payment method, but your grandmother explicitly requested that it not be changed. We're obliged to respect her wishes until she waives her rights."

Of course she did.

Part of him is relieved, infinitely so: he's debt free, his grandmother is taken care of for the remainder of her life, and he's got more than a nest-egg, more than the average middle-class family's expected combined savings even. He could buy a car, a small house, move across the country, pinch the money between his fingers and hunker down in some passable apartment and never work another day in his life but the relief never sinks into his consciousness the way he wishes it would...

Instead it only invites *fear.*

Fear that now he knows the singular entity that knows him and Kim Dan knows him well. Mr. Joo could materialize at any given moment; drift into the hospital emergency lane snug in the massaged leather seats of his white McClaren,

*"Don't worry about compounding interest and fine print Kim Dan,"* he would say, *"Just consider it indentured servitude."*

The thought is horrifying.

It nags in his mouth and makes Dan bite the skin away at his lips, dries the skin around his cuticles, and makes him disassociate at his Grandmother's hospital door everyday he visits her. And today, he could laugh, she mentions the man,

"When will Jaekyung visit? Oh, Dan I do miss him so. Do you know he drove out here to visit me everyday while you were working for his friend? He talks so much once he manages to open up, its so endearing."

Dan smiles weakly at her, he's unimaginably gleeful that the springtime pink of her face has returned but at what cost? He scratches at his wrists before answering, reminding himself that *no,* he was not comatose in Seoul hospital following an knife attack, *yes* he was abroad working for one of Mr. Joo's associates,

"I'm not sure, Grandma," Dan's eyes shake a moment, then, calmly he adds, "I could ask him to if you'd like?"

Could he even though?

In his pocket a phone, purchased by the aforementioned male, is nestled neatly against a butter-soft leather wallet, *also* purchased by the younger man. Maybe Mr. Joo might've punched in his number. Maybe, so that when it came time to collect what he'd lent Dan there would be no confusion on the lendee's part.

With his life's current setup Dan wonders if there's any kind of way to get past this eventuality. There's potential with the small fortune he holds, regardless of whether or not it's his own—for now, or later. He could re-enroll into school, finish a higher tier program, snowball into a specialty that pays more. Certificates are great ways to pull ahead in the working class; often they can be easier to attain depending on what they entail, but he's aware of a few he could even start tonight.

Stocks?

Dan doesn't know a damn thing about stocks but it's nothing he can't learn on Youtube University or with a Masterclass subscription. There are bookstores and libraries that likely offer decades of investment knowledge.

Dan huffs out an exasperated breath, he could gamble it all away like he had in the past. Doing just that had gotten him intertwined with Mr. Joo in the first place, and you've got to be willing to lose as much as your planning to earn in the face of gambling. Mr. Joo, he's an athlete, Dan could always bet on him, or some other athlete, or team, in some other sport, on some other day.

His phone feels heavy in his pocket, weighing him down with its insignificant size and position. It's almost as if Mr. Joo is right there, forcing his body down towards the earth, ready to take and ravage once more,

"That would be nice Dan, could you do that for me?"

He shakes his head and recenters his Grandmother, successfully changing the subject so he can lament the phone later. And inevitably, night befalls the town swiftly and Dan lies across his bed on his back unlocking the phone and opening up the contacts app. Dan sees him there.

Not Joo Jaekyung, or Jaekyung, just *Jae.*

Dan's eyes narrow at the characters on the backlit screen.

He types up a message, thumbs flying across the screen before he can convince himself to do anything else:

**SENT:** *Good evening.*

They hadn't said much when they parted ways, just sealed the deal with a weak handshake, undoing a dog-eared chapter that had finally come to its end. Somehow he finds himself feeling miserable at the sight of Mr. Joo before leaving his penthouse, his eyes sullen and dark, rubbed raw around his waterline and tear ducts. He looked lighter too, taught even, leaner; Dan's eyes scan the phone screen again:

**SENT:** *Good evening.*
***Read at 2223***

And *there.*

The fucking inevitable line, hook, and sinker of regret catches up with him like the same way the edge of a fist catches it's victim's teeth. Dan physically recoils, teeth clacking when he accidentally drops the phone on his face while staring at it like Mr. Joo might jump out of the pixels there and strangle him. His hands fly up to press against his face, positive pressure overlapping the initial pain as it ebbs from sharp to manageable, and finally he's able to pick up the phone again.

**SENT:** *Good evening.*
***Read at 2223***

**Jae:** *hey*

Dan's eyes bounce to the thin HUD along the top of the screen, *2226* it reads in sharp white numbers, and then lower *2223* just above when Dan had sent the message.

Joo Jaekyung reads and answers his message within the sixty seconds of it being sent. Logically it could have also been *less* than sixty seconds, because it can't have been more, otherwise the time would read *2224* and not *2223.*

But what does that mean?

Maybe he's at home, laying in bed the same as Dan, scrolling on his phone when he gets the message and answers it promptly because the phone is literally right there in his hand. The problem with this, however, is that Joo Jaekyung does not doom-scroll. There are far many other things he would busy his time with, namely training, or training, or *training.*

Also, what do you even respond to *hey* with?

His face pinches as he considers the options, but ultimately time pulls on and it's now 2230, if Dan remembers correctly on a good night he falls asleep around 2300. He types then deletes several messages with his lip caught between his teeth. Self-loathing catches up with him and finally Dan settles for:

**SENT:** *Have you been well?*

Because Dan certainly could be, but he has not been because
***Read at 2230***

There, *again.*

Is he just sitting there waiting for the messages? Or is he actually doom-scrolling? A multimillion dollar, high profile athlete, sitting on his phone watching cat videos or whatever else?

**Jae:** *no*

Panic is imminent.

He should not have asked Mr. Joo is he was okay... but why wouldn't he be? He'd won his match and all.

Maybe he'd been using his phone for more nefarious means and——

**SENT:** *Oh, I'm sorry. I shouldn't have asked.*
***Read at 2232***

——*and* he has no business considering those possibilities.

**Jae:** *you didnt do anything wrong*

Okay, that's weird, his tummy swoops awkwardly. For his grandmother, he reminds himself, for her.

**SENT:** *Well, my grandmother has been asking about you, so I was wondering if you would consider dropping in to see her again?*
***Read at 2234***

**Jae:** *sure when*

*When?* For goodness sake, that hadn't been a consideration of Dan's at all; when was a completely reasonably question to ask after being invited to a place.

**SENT:** *I didn't think you were going to say yes... Could I get back to you regarding that?*
***Read at 2235***

**Jae:** *whatever you want*
