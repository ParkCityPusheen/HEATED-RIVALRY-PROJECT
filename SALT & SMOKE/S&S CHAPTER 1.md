# CHAPTER ONE:

It's cold.

About four degrees Celsius.

This cold doesn't keep the citizens of New York from flooding their sidewalks and streets like rats, shuffling to the beat of their individual lives with their chins tucked into their scarves and their gloved fingers hidden in the protection of fabric pockets. Ilya considers some of these well-to-do folks might have pockets as thin as his. Might own a house with walls as weak his. Might survive a life in a perpetual state of collapse, like his.

He pulls on his cigarette absentmindedly, eyes fluttering shut as heated nicotine floods the pitiful air sacs in his lungs. This is his fourth in just two hours, and he should stop, mostly because some bigwig exec is showing face today and not because his bronchial tubes are so inflamed that he suffers symptoms of mild fevers, regularly. He can predict how this man will be just based on the muttering clogging up the empty spaces in the back. Perception is something that has never failed him.

"He's ridiculous," Aimee, a long time hostess of this uptight establishment had hissed, "And for a man who *hates* frivolous conversation he sure does like to bitch and moan his way into unnecessary discussions."

Good thing, Ilya thinks while pulling at the cigarette once more, she hadn't been talking to him, because he wasn't sure what frivolous had meant. But he does now.

Thanks, Google Translate.

There had been other whispers and whines about him, mister owner of these restaurants, and from what Ilya had collected he seemed the reactionary type. None of the staff could agree that he was an outright asshole, but they were quick to bite about his unnecessary hovering and correction. It'd be better if Ilya and Mister Owner never met.

Aimee says it'd be even *better* if Ilya avoided his "hawking" at all costs, actually, since he's unaware that the general manager of this particular location has hired him under the table to not only supply high-grade meats, but also to transport and store.

He ashes the cigarette and flicks the butt to the left of his boot, stamping it out entirely before dropping his head against the brick wall supporting his weight. He's still just a rat in-house and out here, outside of the restaurant in the frigid fucking New York air, he's certain he won't be noticed; maybe he'd pass as some kind of odd penumbra, one whose outline blurred so seamlessly he'd blend right into the steel walls of the kitchen and freezers.

Ilya pulls his phone from his pocket, fifteen till.

He sighs, kicks the cig butt into the street and watches it get flattened, and then pressed into the grooved tires of a heavy sedan that looks more executive than anything he's noticed on wheels in weeks—so it must be his boss's boss.

Great.

He pivots, pulls the staff entrance open to anarchy.

*Definitely* Mister Owner Executive.

Which meant as of now, and for the rest of the season, Ilya wouldn't have to speak to anyone but his boss for the sake of remaining entirely nonexistent.
